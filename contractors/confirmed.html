The warranty in writing

The certificate, the Fix-It Promise, claims and exclusions, before you need them.

The briefing covered what the owner's warranty covers. Here's the rest: the sample certificate, the full Fix-It Promise, how a claim runs and what isn't covered. The full warranty is sent to you in writing before your demo call.

The Fix-It Promise

  • Fixed free, never prorated: seepage is traced to its entry point, sealed by injection, crack fill or patch, and re-treated. Bond failure, surface mold and flooring failure are re-treated; exterior repellency gets the top coat reapplied. Labor and material included.
  • Refund option: if three repairs within 12 months don't fix it, the owner can choose a refund for that area: 100% in the first 24 months, prorated after that (the lifetime bond prorates over 25 years).
  • Renewable: in the last 12 months of a seepage term, a passing Renewal Inspection extends seepage and surface-mold coverage 10 more years. It can be renewed again each time.
  • It outlasts the Certified Contractor: if the original Certified Contractor is no longer in business, WetBond Systems still honors the warranty.
  • Transfers free to each new owner, and the owner never has to buy an inspection, plan or product to keep it.

Applies to residential buildings of one to four units; commercial and multifamily jobs get a separate warranty. Re-treatment needs a bare, penetrable surface, so the owner provides access. Garage Floor Finish coverage is pending its product data sheet. The owner gets the full warranty before signing, and it takes effect when WetBond Systems issues the Warranty Certificate. What isn't covered

  • Water vapor, humidity, condensation and damp spots that dry on their own.
  • Water coming in anywhere other than a treated surface: windows, window wells, sill plates, rim joists, untreated areas.
  • Flooding above ground level, plumbing, appliances, sewer backup and sump pump failure.
  • Structural movement (cracks over 1/8 in, offset, bowing or settling) and pre-existing conditions.
  • Standalone crack or joint repairs, without treating the surface around them.

Also excluded

  • Damage to finishes, flooring, furniture and stored items, and the cost of opening finishes added after the job.
  • Mold on anything that isn't a treated surface: dust, drywall, wood or stored items.
  • Impact, drilling, harsh chemicals, or coatings applied by others over a treated surface.
  • On flatwork: freeze-thaw scaling, deicer damage, cracks and wear.
  • Earthquake, sinkhole, landslide, hurricane and fire.

The contractor's dilemma

"Won't water sit in the wall?"

The objection you'll hear

"If water can't get through, won't it sit in the wall and rot it?"

It's the right worry about coatings, and the wrong one about WetBond. A coating stops water at the face, so water and salts collect behind the film until it blisters off, and as salts crystallize behind a coating they can exert enough pressure to separate it from the masonry.

Coatinga film on the face, with a gap behind it where water and salts collect and push
WetBondreacts inside the pores and bonds there: no gap behind it, nothing to blister, and vapor still passes so the wall dries

Foundation concrete spends its whole life in damp soil. What damages it is water moving through it, carrying minerals out as efflorescence and soaking the surface that freezes. WetBond stops the flow in the concrete without sealing the wall shut.

Drainage vs. WetBond, on site

What a drainage install takes

  • Saw-cut and jackhammer the slab perimeter
  • Haul out broken concrete
  • Lay pipe, set a pit and pump, re-pour
  • Most of the cost is labor
  • A pump that needs power and replacing

What a WetBond job takes

  • Assess and take readings
  • Repair cracks, clean, pre-wet
  • Mix and roll, brush or squeegee
  • Document and register
  • Nothing to service afterward

More questions

The ones that come up on almost every call.

Will it work on block, brick or stone?

WetBond penetrates porous masonry. Block, brick and stone follow their own protocol steps, and denser substrates take more material.

Can I start with floors instead of basements?

Yes. Slab-only Certified Contractors follow the same certification process.
