02Moisture and the pump trap

Today the homeowner gets a drain and a pump, or a coating that peels.

  1. Water doesn't only arrive as a leak.

    Moisture moves through a foundation four ways: liquid flow, capillary suction, vapor diffusion and air movement.9 Drain tile only catches the liquid that reaches the pipe. Interior drainage doesn't stop water from reaching the wall,10 so the concrete stays wet: efflorescence, peeling paint, failed coatings, musty air.

  2. The weakest spot was built in: the cold joint.

    Concrete is poured in stages: footing, then walls, then floor. Where fresh concrete meets concrete that's already cured, it doesn't fully fuse, and that seam is a cold joint. The cove joint, where the wall meets the floor, is one of the most common places water gets into a basement.15 Slabs, foundations, walls and tanks all have cold joints, so this isn't a basement problem. It's a construction problem.

  3. Paints and coatings sit on the face, and water pushes them off.

    A film on the inside of a wet wall is fighting the water from the wrong side. Water and the salts it carries build up behind it, and topical coatings are subject to blistering and failure when they're used to fix basement leaks.16 The homeowner pays twice.

  4. Mechanical systems are machines. Machines fail.

    A sump pump's average service life is about ten years,11 and storms knock the power out exactly when it's needed. A system that depends on a pump is a system with a failure date.

  5. For your crew, both answers cost you.

    If you waterproof basements: drain tile means saw-cut, jackhammer, haul out, lay pipe, set a pump. If you install floor coatings: every slab that fails a moisture test is a job you decline, push back, or coat and hope. When it lifts, it's your callback.

    If you're starting out: the established way into basement waterproofing is the hardest: heavy equipment, big crews, demolition and debris hauling, and competing with companies that have done it for decades.

03How it works

WetBond stops water inside the concrete, not on its face.

Drainage moves water after it's through the wall. WetBond goes into the wall and the floor and stops the water where it enters. Start with one brick.

How WetBond stops it, in three moves

  1. Go on wet. React inside the concrete.

    Most coatings need dry concrete. WetBond needs water. The wall is pre-wetted, then WetBond drives into the concrete and reacts with the water inside it. It bonds into the substrate instead of sitting on the face, and it's engineered to let vapor through slowly (about 1.1 perm) so pressure releases instead of building behind a film. We know of no other system that changes the concrete itself.

  2. Treat walls, cold joints and floor as one envelope.

    Cracks and cold joints are routed and filled first, and Injection Gel goes in wherever water is actively running. Then walls, cove and floor go on as one continuous envelope. About one day to apply to a typical basement; prep and repairs vary by job.

  3. Turn every job into evidence.

    Readings, photos and batch numbers before, during and after. Certified, registered jobs carry a limited manufacturer warranty: a lifetime bond and renewable seepage coverage. A contractor who bought a pail somewhere can't promise that. Certification is what makes it yours to offer.

A new category: substrate conversion

WetBond doesn't coat the concrete or drain it. It converts the surface zone of the concrete itself, using a water-reactive material that pulls into porous surfaces, reacts and forms chemical bonds there.

Water-reactive

Goes on damp concrete and uses the water already in the pores to react, where coatings need the surface dry.

Chemically bonded

Bonds into the substrate instead of sitting on it, so there's no film to lift, blister or peel.

Stops water, passes vapor

Stops liquid water and moisture while letting vapor through slowly, about 1.1 perm, so the wall can still dry.

The whole system is water-reactive

Our crack filler and Injection Gel work the same way: water-reactive and chemically bonding, properties that are rare in repair materials, and formulated to work with WetBond so the repairs are part of the warranty, not a weak point in it.

    Questions contractors ask

    Before your call.

    "You can't waterproof from the inside."

    Coatings on the inside face usually fail because moisture pushes them off. WetBond doesn't sit on the face: it goes onto a pre-wetted wall and reacts with water inside the concrete. Where water rises through the floor or the water table is high, drainage still has a job, and the assessment says so.

    "Nothing sticks to a wet slab."

    Most coatings need dry concrete. WetBond needs moisture: the slab is pre-wetted so it's evenly damp before application. Finishes go on over it per the protocol.

    What about hydrostatic pressure?

    Active leaks get Injection Gel and crack repair first. WetBond is engineered to let vapor through slowly so pressure releases instead of building behind a film. In high water tables, drainage is part of the answer too.

    I'm new to the trades. Can I start?

    Yes, if you'll run a crew and do the work. Certification covers the protocol from assessment to registration, and your first two to three documented jobs are reviewed and audited. You'll need licensing and insurance as your state requires.

    I've never sold this kind of work. Will I get help?

    Yes, in the form of training and tools, not promises. Certification covers the protocol, the Certified Contractor Academy includes sales and customer-service manuals, and your first two to three jobs are reviewed and audited with you. The moisture inspection does much of the explaining: you show the owner the readings, the map and what you recommend, including when the site needs drainage too. We don't promise leads, sales or income.

    Do you provide leads?

    We market WetBond to homeowners, but we don't sell or promise leads. Every Certified Contractor gets our lead-generation best practices, and our marketing partner offers done-for-you lead generation as a separate, optional service.

    Is financing available?

    Yes. Financing is available toward the startup investment for contractors who qualify, and for your customers' jobs, through third-party lenders and subject to credit approval. Terms vary by lender and applicant; we'll go over the options on your demo call.

    Can I finish the floor or wall after waterproofing?

    Yes. WetBond flooring top coats and pigmented wall top-coat systems are compatible with the waterproofing, so you can deliver a finished surface for owners who care about the look.

    What equipment do I need?

    A grinder and vacuum, mixing drill, rollers and squeegees, a dual-cartridge crack gun with compressor, and a moisture meter. We don't sell equipment: buy it from any supplier, or use your own if it meets spec. We'll show you where to get every piece, and equipment financing is available if you qualify.

    My crew doesn't do waterproofing.

    If your crew can prep and roll concrete, it can be certified: online training, a written exam and a review and audit of your first two to three documented jobs. Hands-on instruction is an optional paid add-on.

    Is this a franchise?

    No. You run your own company under your own brand, with optional co-branded materials for certified contractors. There's no franchise fee, territory fee or royalty, and marketing, software and the Certified Contractor Academy are optional.

    Won't it trap water in the wall?

    No. Coatings trap water because they sit on the face with a gap behind them. WetBond reacts and bonds inside the pores, so there's no gap for water to collect in, and it lets vapor through slowly so the wall can dry.

    Do you treat exterior flatwork?

    Yes. Driveways, patios, walkways and pool decks are an anchor job, alongside basements and interior slabs.

    How much will I make?

    We don't make income claims. Running a business takes knowledge, skill and perseverance, and results vary with your background, your drive and your market, as well as your crew, your sales and correct application. Run the math on your own jobs and set your own prices.
